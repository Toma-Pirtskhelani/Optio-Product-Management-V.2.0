Ask a team if their new AI coding habit is helping. Most say yes. Ask how they know, and you usually get a feeling, not a number.

Ihor Segodin, our Java Team Lead, started Strikersoft's shared library of AI coding "skills" and wanted proof, not just a feeling, that they worked. That's what led Andrii Vasyliev, one of our Java engineers, to build a harness that runs the same coding task with and without a given skill, then scores the result. One run: 100% pass with the skill loaded, 78% without.
That's the engineering side of the answer. But the same logic scales up to how we govern AI use as a whole.

Why measure instead of just writing the rule and moving on?

Michael Purnyn put it well: ISO-compliant AI governance isn't a checkbox, it's a routine. Test models repeatedly, track the results, and you catch a model underperforming before it hits real users.

Skill evolution: don't just write down what worked, keep proving it still does.

Every minute counts when someone is trapped under rubble. Smoke fills the air, structures collapse into darkness, and the signals rescue teams normally rely on stop working. Detecting a heartbeat in those conditions takes different technology than detecting one in daylight.

That's what we work on in Cynergy4MIE and NeAIxt Project: human detection and vital signs monitoring in visually impaired environments, using sensors mounted on unmanned ground and air vehicles. Cynergy4MIE uses mmWave sensors. NeAIxt adds infrared sensors from IRnova.

Here's where our role comes in: we're almost two-thirds into Cynergy4MIE, and part of that work overlaps with NeAIxt, fusing our mmWave radar with newly added infrared sensors. Together with IRnova and KTH Royal Institute of Technology, we integrate the AI pipelines and validate that detection holds up under real-world conditions, not just in a lab.

This work runs deeper than one project. It's part of what led us to set up Sindri Logic AB, a company built to take dual-use sensing further.

If your team is working on similar sensing or detection challenges, we'd be glad to compare notes.

Everyone asks whether #AI makes developers faster, but almost nobody asks how they actually know.

We measure it the boring way. Estimates before every task, time tracked in #Jira, defects per version, and retros whenever something doesn't add up. No magic framework, just the same discipline we've used for twenty years pointed at a new variable.

The hard part turned out not to be the metrics at all, but the adjustment: learning to treat your way of working with AI as something shared and visible, not personal and private.

We're putting together a full #article on how we measure and what we've learned so far. Coming soon.
